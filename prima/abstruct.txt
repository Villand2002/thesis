\section{abstruct}
This paper studies student admissions with overlapping reserve categories. In many affirmative action and diversity-based admissions systems, students may be eligible for more than one reserved category, while each admitted student can ultimately be counted toward only one seat category. This creates a nontrivial allocation problem: the category to which an overlapping student is assigned affects both the use of reserved seats and the fairness of the final outcome.

We propose a score-elevated overlapping reserve mechanism. Each student has an original score, a set of eligible reserved categories, and preferences over seat categories. The open category is processed according to the original scores, while each reserved category is processed according to a category-specific adjusted score. For a reserved category (t), a student’s adjusted score is defined as the original score plus a category-specific bonus (k_t) if the student is eligible for category (t). Thus, eligibility for a reserved category is incorporated as a score advantage within that category, rather than as a hard exclusion rule. The mechanism sequentially assigns open seats first and then reserved categories according to a fixed processing order. Once a student is assigned to a category, the student is removed from later consideration, implementing one-to-one counting.

The paper also discusses the appropriate feasibility and fairness notions for this environment. In particular, standard score-feasibility based only on original-score cutoffs is not directly suitable when reserved categories use score elevation. We therefore formulate feasibility in terms of score displacement: a lower original-score student may displace a higher original-score student only when the displacement is justified by the category-specific score elevation and the original-score gap remains bounded. We also define fairness within a category using the adjusted score relevant to that category. Under this view, a matching is unfair if an unmatched student has a higher adjusted score than a student assigned to the same reserved category.

This approach shifts the design of reserve policies from hard category-based eligibility rules to category-specific priority design. It is especially useful in overlapping-category environments because it avoids the need to decide ex ante which single reserved identity should determine a student’s priority. Instead, different categories can recognize different forms of eligibility through their own score adjustments, while the final assignment still counts each admitted student only once. The proposed framework clarifies how score-based affirmative action policies can be extended to overlapping reserve categories and provides a basis for analyzing their fairness and feasibility properties.
